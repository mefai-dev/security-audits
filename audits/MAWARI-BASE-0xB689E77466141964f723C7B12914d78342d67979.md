## Why This Report Exists

Mawari is marketed as a DePIN (decentralized physical infrastructure network) for real time 3D and spatial computing, an immersive streaming layer served by a decentralized network of Guardian nodes that, per the pitch, render and stream 3D content. The project is real, funded (roughly 10.8M USD reported by Forbes in 2024, up to 17.8M USD total per RootData and CryptoRank, lead investor 1kx), and has run a Guardian Node license sale with Republic under a Reg D wrapper. Its marketing cites enterprise relationships (Deutsche Telekom, KDDI, Telefonica, Magic Leap, Netflix, Qualcomm, T-Mobile).

The question this report answers is narrow and technical: when you read the actual public source and the actual on chain state, is there a genuine decentralized rendering and streaming network with trustless on chain proof of work, or is there a centralized, closed source streaming product wrapped in an on chain reward and license layer. The two are very different risk profiles, and the marketing does not distinguish them.

## The Foundation: a real company, a closed core, and a testnet economic layer

Mawari the company is real and has shipped an XR streaming product for years. That product, the split rendering engine and the fleet described in marketing as thousands of GPUs across APAC, is closed source and not present in any public repository. The public GitHub org contains exactly two repositories, and neither is the node, the renderer or the streaming engine:

- `NASS-TOOL` (Next.js, TypeScript) self describes in `config/wagmi.ts` as the "NASS Tool" for "Mawari License Staking & Delegation." It is a wallet front end whose flow is, verbatim from its README, "Connect wallet, Approve escrow, Deposit & delegate licenses."
- `Automated-Scripts` (JavaScript) is operations tooling: token URI and metadata scripts, `ipfs-data.json`, and `delegations.csv` and `licenses.csv` batch data. Peripheral, not protocol code.

The on chain machinery that the license tool talks to is real and deployed, but on the Mawari Orbit testnet (chainId 576), which is live and busy (5,283,529 blocks, 12,281,334 transactions, 15,258,466 addresses at audit time). From `lib/constant.ts` (with `IS_TESTNET: true`, `SERVER_URL: http://localhost:3000`), the contract set is:

Two structural facts frame everything below. First, all five economic contracts are upgradeable `EIP-1967` proxies whose implementation source is unverified on the testnet explorer, so the actual logic is bytecode only. Second, the mainnet Orbit chain (chainId 629275, Caldera hosted, Multicall3 `0x3F1BD1Abc350eD6313Ff7Eaab561DCAbbcc61071` in `config/chains.ts`) is not publicly reachable: the documented RPC `mawari-network-mainnet.rpc.caldera.xyz` and the matching Caldera explorer both return HTTP 404. The auditable, live version of the node economy is therefore the testnet, not mainnet.

## Claim 1: A decentralized network of Guardian nodes that render and stream 3D content

CLAIM. Marketing and aggregator copy present Guardian nodes as the decentralized compute that renders and streams immersive 3D content, for example CryptoRank describing "distributed GPU nodes called Guardian Nodes."

REALITY: OVERSTATED, with a false element. Guardian nodes do not render or stream. Mawari's own documentation and blog contradict the marketing.

EVIDENCE. blog.mawari.net Guardian Nodes Explained states verbatim: "Guardian Nodes are specialized, CPU-based validators that monitor and verify the performance of the Mawari Network," and that their job is "Verifying the results of rendering tasks completed by Spatial Streamer Nodes," tracking "latency, jitter, packet loss, and video quality." The docs Guardian Node license page reduces the operator duty to availability: "a minimum of 8 distributed availability verification cycles ('heartbeat tasks')" per 24 hours. Rendering is attributed to a separate class, Spatial Streamer Nodes, and the documentation does not establish that those are permissionless third party operators rather than Mawari operated GPU infrastructure. The public GitHub org contains no renderer, no streamer node and no Guardian node binary, so the split rendering engine cannot be verified at all. CryptoRank calling Guardian nodes "GPU nodes" directly contradicts Mawari's own "CPU-based" description, evidence of the conflation.

IMPACT. HIGH. The single most repeated selling point, a decentralized network that renders and streams, is not what the public Guardian node program is. Guardians are a CPU monitoring and quality assurance layer. The rendering that gives the token its narrative is closed source and, on the available evidence, centralized.

## Claim 2: On chain node license registration, escrow, staking and delegation

CLAIM. Operators buy Guardian Node licenses, stake and delegate them, and the network tracks this on chain.

REALITY: CONFIRMED on testnet. This part is real and independently verifiable.

EVIDENCE. `lib/constant.ts` and `lib/abi/` wire a coherent system: an ERC1155 license (`LICENSE_1155`), an escrow (`LICENSE_ESCROW`), a delegation contract (`DELEGATION`) and a rewards epoch configuration (`REWARDS_EPOCH_CONFIG`), all deployed and returning live bytecode on Mawari testnet 576. `config/wagmi.ts` labels the app "Mawari License Staking & Delegation," and the README flow "Approve escrow, Deposit & delegate licenses" matches the escrow plus delegation contracts. This is a genuine on chain license and delegation primitive, not vaporware.

IMPACT. INFO, trending positive. A real, inspectable license and delegation layer exists. The caveats that lower its weight are that it is testnet, the implementations are unverified bytecode, and the contracts are upgradeable proxies (see Claim 4), and that the corresponding mainnet deployment is not publicly reachable (see The Foundation).

## Claim 3: Node operators earn rewards for real streaming and rendering work

CLAIM. The DePIN pitch implies operators are paid for contributing streaming and rendering compute. Docs promise activation rewards worth 11 percent of total supply over three years and network activity rewards from 20 percent of net revenue.

REALITY: OVERSTATED. Rewards are gated on license ownership and uptime heartbeats, not on measured rendering or streaming output, and the amounts are fixed schedules and revenue shares rather than pay for compute.

EVIDENCE. The docs activation rewards page pays per license by tier (for example 10 tokens per license for 1 to 9 licenses, up to 200 per license at 500 or more) and requires only "8 heartbeat verification cycles" per day to qualify, with a lock of "70% of previously claimed eligible rewards" to keep receiving tranches. The reward schedule is time based (monthly over 12 months, "mainnet launch" onward) and a revenue share, not a function of rendered frames or streamed bytes. On chain, the `REWARDS_EPOCH_CONFIG` and `ORACLE` contracts drive distribution from operator supplied heartbeat and quality metrics, which is uptime and quality attestation, not proof of rendering work.

IMPACT. MEDIUM. Operators should understand they are paid to keep a monitoring node online and to hold licenses, not to sell rendering compute. The economics are closer to a staking and uptime incentive than to a compute marketplace.

## Claim 4: On chain, trustless proof of streaming and rendering

CLAIM. As a DePIN, the implication is that useful work (rendering, streaming) is proven on chain and rewards follow trustlessly.

REALITY: FALSE. There is no on chain proof of rendering or streaming, and the reward path depends on a single externally owned account.

EVIDENCE. The only performance signal that reaches the chain arrives through `ORACLE` (`0xDD07b108b9d1c1A133345e83F5C8d25dbBEA9963`), whose `owner()` resolves to `0x000ca94460b2f8bfdf923af7048219edd57f46f4`, and `eth_getCode` on that address returns zero bytes, meaning it is a plain EOA, not a multisig or timelock. Whoever holds that key can set the performance data that determines who is a good node and who gets paid. All the economic contracts are upgradeable `EIP-1967` proxies with unverified implementations, so their reward logic cannot be read and can be swapped by the proxy admin. No contract in the set validates a render output, a stream, or any cryptographic proof of media work. The rendering engine that would produce such proofs is closed source and absent from GitHub.

IMPACT. HIGH. The trust model is centralized. Rewards flow from an admin controlled oracle over upgradeable, unverified contracts. This is a permissioned reward system with a decentralization narrative, not a trustless DePIN.

## Claim 5: A live decentralized network of roughly 180,000 nodes

CLAIM. Community and blog copy cite "180,000 nodes" powering a global compute network.

REALITY: OVERSTATED and not independently verifiable. The figure conflates cumulative testnet participants with active, decentralized, revenue serving nodes.

EVIDENCE. The Mawari testnet shows 15.26 million addresses and 12.28 million transactions, consistent with a large, incentivized testnet campaign that generates many heartbeat transactions and many one time addresses. There is no public, verifiable registry that resolves "180,000 nodes" into distinct, active, mainnet Guardian operators serving real demand, and the mainnet chain endpoint is not reachable to check. A busy testnet is not proof of a live decentralized production network.

IMPACT. MEDIUM. Headline node counts should be read as testnet or campaign participation, not as an audited count of production infrastructure.

## Claim 6: MAWARI is a live, tradeable utility token with a real market

CLAIM. MAWARI is the network utility and reward token, presented as a tradeable asset.

REALITY: OVERSTATED. The token is deployed and technically transferable, but it is effectively untraded on chain and heavily concentrated.

EVIDENCE. On chain: DexScreener returns zero pairs for `0xB689E77466141964f723C7B12914d78342d67979`; GeckoTerminal indexes a single MAWARI, USDC pool holding about 0.07 USD of liquidity with 0 USD of 24 hour volume; CoinGecko, CoinMarketCap and DropsTab have no market data (DropsTab: "Market data is not available yet"). Blockscout counts only 1,606 transfers among 691 holders. Distribution is extreme: the top five holders are EOAs holding exactly 160,000,000 MAWARI each (13.33 percent each, 800,000,000 combined, or 66.7 percent of supply), none of them recognizable vesting or timelock contracts, and the deployer EOA `0xeFC022B49c82Eb4bF288Ccd0B139ea753BDa981b` holds a further 36 million. This is a pre liquidity, treasury and allocation held distribution, not a token with an active market.

IMPACT. MEDIUM. Any secondary trading is nascent and, if present, likely centralized exchange only. Two thirds of supply sits in a handful of plain wallets, so distribution and unlock behavior, not smart contract exploits, are the dominant token risk.

## Claim 7: Token contract safety, fixed supply and no admin or mint backdoor

CLAIM. Implicit safety claim for the ERC20 itself.

REALITY: CONFIRMED, and this is the strongest technical positive.

EVIDENCE. The Base token exposes no `owner()`, no `DEFAULT_ADMIN_ROLE`, no `MINTER_ROLE`, no `paused()` and no `permit` or votes surface (every such `eth_call` reverts). Both the `EIP-1967` implementation and admin storage slots are empty, so it is not an upgradeable proxy. Total supply is a fixed 1,200,000,000 with 18 decimals. In plain terms, there is no visible function to mint new supply, pause transfers, or upgrade the logic. The main deduction is that the source is unverified on Base Blockscout, so this assurance rests on bytecode behavior rather than published Solidity.

IMPACT. POSITIVE. The token itself carries no observable inflation or pause backdoor, which materially lowers the smart contract rug vector even though distribution risk (Claim 6) remains high.

## Claim 8: An open and verifiable DePIN

CLAIM. DePIN framing implies openness and verifiability.

REALITY: FALSE as stated. The core is closed and the mainnet is not publicly verifiable.

EVIDENCE. The public GitHub org holds two peripheral repositories and no node, renderer, streamer or protocol contract source. Every economic contract implementation on testnet is unverified bytecode. The mainnet Orbit chain RPC and explorer both return 404. The Base token source is unverified. Taken together, the parts a third party can independently read are a license front end, some operations scripts, and the external behavior of a fixed supply ERC20. The rest is closed or unreachable.

IMPACT. HIGH transparency finding. Per audit policy, the closed and unverifiable core caps the score and is flagged. Unseen claims about rendering, streaming and mainnet decentralization are not credited.

## Positive Findings

- The Base MAWARI token is a clean, fixed supply ERC20 with no observable owner, mint, pause or upgrade backdoor (Claim 7). This is the single most reassuring on chain fact.
- A genuine on chain license, escrow, delegation and rewards epoch system exists and is live on the Mawari testnet, wired to an open source tool (`NASS-TOOL`). The primitives are real, not slideware (Claim 2).
- Mawari is a real, funded company with a multi year XR product history and named enterprise relationships. This is not an anonymous launch.
- Mawari's own documentation is honest that Guardian nodes are CPU monitoring and verification nodes, which is what an auditor needs to separate the technical reality from third party marketing.

## Conclusion

Mawari is a real company shipping a closed source, and on the available evidence centralized, XR streaming product, wrapped in a genuine but early on chain incentive layer. When you read the only public source and the on chain state, the flagship DePIN claim does not survive intact. Guardian nodes monitor and attest quality with CPU heartbeats; they do not render or stream. The rendering that anchors the story is closed source and absent from any repository. Rewards are gated on license ownership and uptime and are driven by an oracle that resolves to a single EOA over upgradeable, unverified proxy contracts, so the reward path is permissioned, not trustless. The verifiable, live version of the node economy is a busy testnet; the documented mainnet chain is not publicly reachable. The token contract itself is commendably clean, fixed supply with no admin or mint backdoor, but it is untraded on chain (zero DEX liquidity, no aggregator market data) and two thirds of supply sits in five plain wallets.

None of this proves fraud, and the clean token plus real testnet infrastructure keep this out of high risk territory. But the core is closed and unverifiable, the headline decentralization is overstated, and there is no on chain proof of the physical work the DePIN is supposed to decentralize. Under a source first audit, a closed core caps the score and flags transparency. Score 42 of 100, Flagged, risk MEDIUM. Watch items: source verification of the Base token and the Orbit chain contracts, a reachable and verifiable mainnet, migration of the reward oracle from an EOA to a multisig or timelock, and real distribution of the concentrated supply.
