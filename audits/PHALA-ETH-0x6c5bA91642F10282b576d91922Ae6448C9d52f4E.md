## Executive Summary

Phala Network is a real confidential compute project with a verified token and, unusually for this sector, genuine paying usage. It is not vaporware. The MEDIUM rating reflects a central contradiction in its "zero trust" marketing and a centralizing trajectory:

1. **"Zero trust" is contradicted by hardware vendor dependence.** Phala markets a system that "does not trust any hardware provider," yet its entire verifiability chain terminates in Intel and NVIDIA silicon and their attestation services. It relocates trust from cloud operators to two chip vendors, it does not remove it.
2. **The "tamper proof" framing omits demonstrated hardware weaknesses.** Trusted execution environments of the type Phala relies on have documented side channel attacks, including a 2025 physical access key extraction attack, none of which is hedged on Phala's own pages.
3. **The original decentralized identity is being wound down:** the Kusama/Polkadot parachains have been sunset in favor of an Ethereum L2 plus a foundation run confidential AI cloud service, with a corporate rebrand.
4. Real usage exists (confidential inference, paying customers) but the headline figures are self reported.

The technology and usage are real; the caution is the zero trust overclaim, the TEE threat model silence and the centralizing pivot.

## 3. Claim vs Reality: "Zero Trust" / "Does Not Trust Any Hardware Provider"

> Site / docs: "Private execution. Verifiable results."; "Phala does not trust any cloud platform, hardware provider, or even its users"; "Every result can carry proof."

**Reality: trust is relocated to Intel and NVIDIA, not removed.** Phala's own materials concede that endorsements and attestation "from Intel and NVIDIA provide an additional layer of trust." The entire verifiability chain terminates in **Intel SGX/TDX and NVIDIA GPU attestation** and those vendors' remote attestation infrastructure. "Verifiable" therefore means "verifiable if you trust Intel and NVIDIA root keys and that the enclave is not side channeled", which directly contradicts the "does not trust any hardware provider" claim.

---

## 4. Claim vs Reality: "Tamper Proof" vs Demonstrated TEE Breaks

Phala presents attestation as effectively absolute ("tamper proof", "prove what ran"). In reality, trusted execution environments of the type it relies on have **documented side channel attacks**, including a 2025 physical access attack that extracted secrets and keys from mainstream TEEs using inexpensive hardware. Phala's promotional pages contain **no threat model discussion** of side channels or vendor compromise. The confidentiality guarantee is conditional and breakable, not absolute.

---

## 5. Claim vs Reality: Decentralization Trajectory and Usage

- **Centralizing pivot:** the original decentralization narrative (Kusama and Polkadot parachains, tens of thousands of miners) is being wound down, the parachains have been sunset in favor of an **Ethereum L2 plus a foundation run confidential AI cloud** with paid tiers and a corporate rebrand. Trust concentrates increasingly in a Phala operated cloud plus the hardware vendors.
- **Usage (a genuine positive, self reported):** unlike many crypto AI projects, Phala reports plausible real throughput (confidential inference serving a large third party model router) and paying customers. The main caveat is that headline figures (daily token throughput, annual revenue, paid user counts) come from Phala's own reporting, not an independent audit.

## 9. Conclusion

Phala Network is a real confidential compute project with a verified, capped token and, unusually, genuine paying usage, which keeps it in the MEDIUM band at 60/100. It is held back because its "zero trust, does not trust any hardware provider" claim is contradicted by a verifiability chain that terminates in Intel and NVIDIA attestation, because its "tamper proof" framing omits documented TEE side channel attacks, and because its original decentralized identity is being wound down in favor of an Ethereum L2 plus a foundation run cloud SaaS. The technology and usage are real; the caution is the trust model overclaim and the centralizing pivot.

---

## 10. Recommendations

**For the Phala team:**
- Reframe "zero trust" honestly: trust is relocated to Intel and NVIDIA attestation, not eliminated; disclose this dependency.
- Add a threat model section addressing TEE side channel and physical access attacks, rather than presenting attestation as absolute.
- Be transparent about the pivot from parachains to an L2 plus cloud SaaS, and publish independently verifiable usage figures.

**For users:**
- Understand "verifiable / tamper proof" is conditional on trusting chip vendor attestation and on the absence of side channel compromise.
- Note PHA is capped at 1 billion but still inflating, and that the network is centralizing.
