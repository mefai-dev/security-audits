## Executive Summary

Power Ledger is a real, operating energy technology company, and its POWR token contract is one of the cleaner ERC 20s we review: an immutable 2017 deployment with a fixed supply, no mint path, no owner over funds, and no upgrade proxy. The token itself carries almost no contract level risk. The gap in this audit sits at the project and claim level, where the marketing still leads with a flagship peer to peer energy trading vision that remains pilot stage roughly eight years after the ICO, while the token utility that once justified holding POWR has been materially weakened.

1. **The contract is clean and immutable.** MEFAI's direct read confirms a hard coded supply of exactly one billion POWR at 6 decimals, no mint or emission path, no owner or controller, no pause, no fee on transfer, and no proxy. Supply and balances cannot be altered by any party.
2. **One genuinely commercial product exists, and it is not the flagship.** TraceX, the renewable energy certificate marketplace, is live and integrated with real registries including M RETS and ERCOT in Texas. The headline peer to peer energy trading engine, Transactive, remains largely at pilot, trial and R and D stage.
3. **Token utility has weakened.** Power Ledger deprecated its own Energy Blockchain and its staking program during the October 2024 dual chain expansion to Solana. POWR now exists mainly as a bridged ERC 20 and SPL token, and the revenue generating product settles in conventional fiat rather than requiring POWR.
4. **Real clients, pilot framing.** The named institutions check out as genuine energy sector organisations, which is a real positive, but the client roster mixes historical trials with live deployments and the messaging frames pilot stage work as commercial momentum.

The token is not a scam, and both the company and the TraceX marketplace are real and operating. The caution is that POWR utility is now limited and loosely coupled to the actual commercial business, and the flagship vision remains pre commercial. This lands Power Ledger at 66 out of 100, Passed with a Medium risk rating.

## 2. On chain Security Assessment (MEFAI analysis)

MEFAI's direct read of the POWR contract on Ethereum returned:

| Check | Result |
|-------|--------|
| Token identity | PowerLedger, POWR, 6 decimals, version 1.0, verified |
| Total supply | 1,000,000,000 POWR (1000000000000000 base units at 6 decimals), fixed constant |
| Mint authority | None; no mint, addMinter or emission path exists (selectors revert) |
| Owner / controller / admin | None; those selectors revert with an invalid opcode |
| Pause authority | None; not pausable or freezable |
| Upgradeable | No; the contract is not a proxy |
| Transfer fee | None; transfers move value one to one |
| Only privileged role | migrationInfoSetter at `0xd66873c0d33e27a1fd902eea663c285aecd192c1`, an externally owned account, whose sole power is writing an informational migration string; that string is currently empty |

**Interpretation.** At the contract level POWR is genuinely low risk. The entire supply was assigned to the deployer in the constructor with no later minting path, so inflation is impossible, and the absence of any owner, controller or pause means no party can freeze or seize holder balances. The one residual contract concern is minor: the migrationInfoSetter is presently a plain externally owned account rather than the multisig the source comment intends, so a key compromise could post a false onchain migration announcement usable for phishing, though it can never mint, move or freeze tokens. The contract also uses legacy behavior where transfer and transferFrom return false instead of reverting on failure and predates SafeMath, which some modern integrations may mishandle. Neither of these touches custody. The substantive questions for this project sit at the claim and product level below, not in the token contract.

## 3. Claim vs Reality: "Peer to Peer Energy Trading" as the Flagship

> Site: "P2P Energy Trading" and "Introducing Transactive Lite: A Faster Path To Peer to Peer Energy Trading," under the banner "The power behind new energy."

**Reality: the flagship vision is still pilot stage.** Peer to peer energy trading is the story Power Ledger has led with since its 2017 ICO, but roughly eight years later the Transactive engine that powers it remains largely at pilot, trial and R and D stage across utility programs rather than a fully commercial, at scale product. The genuinely live, commercially operating product is a different one, the TraceX renewable energy certificate marketplace. The company's own 2025 to 2026 narrative openly frames the goal as converting pilots into commercial deployments, which itself implies most peer to peer work is not yet commercial. This is not a broken product, it is a real but pre commercial flagship being presented as the headline of a working business.

---

## 4. Claim vs Reality: POWR Utility, Staking and the Solana Move

> Site: "Powerledger is now integrated with Solana Mainnet," POWR holders can "seamlessly onramp, offramp, access, and trade POWR tokens," and an "Unstake POWR" action pointing to stake.powerledger.io.

**Reality: utility exists on paper but has been materially weakened.** The design is a dual token model where POWR is the access and staking token that application hosts stake to use the platform, and Sparkz is a fiat pegged unit that settles energy trades locally. That utility is now much thinner than the branding implies, because Power Ledger deprecated its own Energy Blockchain and its staking program during the October 2024 dual chain expansion to Solana. POWR now exists mainly as an ERC 20 and a Solana SPL token connected by Wormhole bridging, and the genuinely revenue generating product, TraceX certificate trading, runs on conventional fiat settlement and does not clearly require customers to hold or spend POWR. The token is therefore loosely coupled to the actual commercial business, which leaves POWR closer to a speculative asset than an indispensable platform token. The surviving onchain footprint of the migration, the migration announcement field on this contract, is currently empty, so nothing is signaled or enforced by the ERC 20 itself.

## 5. Claim vs Reality: Partnerships and the Client Roster

> Site: "Powerledger facilitated the largest peer to peer (P2P) trading project with over 1,000 participants."

**Reality: real institutions, but pilots framed as momentum.** This is where Power Ledger earns real credit. The named partners and clients check out as genuine, well known energy sector organisations rather than fabricated logos, including Energie Steiermark in Austria, BCPG MEA and TDED in Thailand, Tata Power DDL and CESC in India, KEPCO in Japan, Elia in Belgium and Metlen in Greece. The caveat is presentational: the roster mixes live commercial deployments with historical pilots and short trials, for example earlier Australian projects such as RENeW Nexus and CUB, an eight month SEDA trial in Malaysia and early phase KEPCO work, without always flagging which engagements are ongoing versus concluded. Superlatives such as the world's largest peer to peer energy trading pilot are still descriptions of pilots. The counterparties are real, which is a meaningful positive, but the framing presents pilot stage traction as commercial momentum.

---

## 6. Claim vs Reality: Supply and Token Facts

> Site: POWR is presented as an energy platform utility token with a supply on the order of one billion.

**Reality: the token facts match the chain exactly.** Unlike the product narrative, the hard token claims are accurate. The marketed supply of roughly one billion POWR matches the onchain constant precisely at 1,000,000,000, with circulating supply near the full total at about 999.5M per market trackers. There is no hidden mint, no reserve mechanism, and no upgrade path that could change these numbers. On the specific, verifiable question of token supply and controls, Power Ledger is transparent and its claims hold.

## 11. Conclusion

Power Ledger is a real energy technology company with a genuinely clean token contract, which is why it passes. The POWR ERC 20 is immutable, fixed supply, and free of mint, owner, pause and proxy risk, so holder balances cannot be inflated or seized onchain, and the token supply matches the marketing exactly. Where the picture softens is the project narrative: the flagship peer to peer energy trading vision remains pilot stage roughly eight years after the ICO, the one fully commercial product is the TraceX certificate marketplace rather than the headline, and POWR utility was materially weakened when staking and the own Energy Blockchain were retired in the October 2024 Solana migration. The client roster is real, which counts in the project's favor, but it presents pilot stage work as commercial momentum. None of this is a contract exploit or a fabricated business. It is a legitimate company whose token utility now lags its branding. Power Ledger scores 66 out of 100, Passed, with a Medium overall risk rating.

---

## 12. Recommendations

**For the Power Ledger team:**
- Clearly separate the live commercial product (TraceX) from the pilot stage flagship (peer to peer energy trading) in marketing, and label engagements as ongoing versus concluded.
- Restore a concrete, current utility role for POWR in the revenue generating product, or stop implying the token powers a business it is now loosely coupled to.
- Move the migrationInfoSetter to a multisig so a single key compromise cannot post a false onchain migration announcement.
- Present pilot metrics as pilots, and reserve commercial framing for deployments that are actually generating revenue.

**For users:**
- Recognize that POWR utility is limited today: staking was deprecated in the October 2024 Solana migration and the revenue product does not clearly require the token.
- Understand that the flagship peer to peer energy trading vision is still pre commercial, while TraceX is the product that is genuinely live.
- Note the low contract level risk (immutable, fixed supply, no mint, no owner), but treat any onchain migration message with caution given the setter is a single externally owned key.
- Verify which client engagements are live versus historical before treating the roster as current traction.
