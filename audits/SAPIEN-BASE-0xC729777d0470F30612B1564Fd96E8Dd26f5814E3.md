## Why This Report Exists

Sapien is marketed as a decentralized human data network: a permissionless protocol where a global crowd labels and validates AI training data and earns tokens, with the frequent claim that the whole loop (points, rewards, staked tokens, reputation) is "secured on chain" on Base. That framing implies the core intelligence of the system, deciding what work is good and who gets paid, is verifiable smart contract logic. This report reads the actual public source and the live Base state to separate what is genuinely enforced by code from what is an off chain web platform with an on chain settlement layer. The token is treated as secondary but is fully characterized from chain state.

## Method

Read only and public. Steps taken:

1. Confirmed the token identity directly from Base RPC (`eth_call` for `name`, `symbol`, `decimals`, `totalSupply`, `maxSupply`) and disambiguated it from the unrelated Ethereum "SPN" token.
2. Inspected the token runtime bytecode for privileged selectors (mint, roles, ownership, pause, upgrade proxy slots) rather than trusting a listing page.
3. Cloned and read the public `Sapien-io/sapien-contracts` repo in full: `SapienVault.sol` (631 lines), `Types.sol`, the interface, the design doc `docs/SapienVault.md`, the `AGENTS.md` working notes, the deploy scripts, and the in repo audit finding trackers.
4. Verified the vault on chain: proxy type, implementation address, `asset`, `totalAssets`, share supply, pause state, and the admin identity, then resolved the admin address as a Gnosis Safe and read its threshold and owners.
5. Cross checked verification status and token metadata on a public Base explorer, and reviewed public market and platform traction claims.

Market and platform traction numbers are off chain marketing inputs and are labeled as such; they are not credited as code facts.

## The Foundation: A Centralized Platform With an On Chain Staking and Slashing Layer

The single most important structural fact, which the project itself states plainly in its own documentation, is that the decision making core is off chain. From `docs/SapienVault.md`:

> "SapienVault is the economic security layer for Sapien's Proof of Quality (PoQ) network, purely an asset locking and slashing mechanism. It does not score, evaluate, or judge contribution quality on chain; it only escrows collateral and applies penalties when instructed."

> "The validation work itself lives off chain in the PoQ engine: a server (or set of servers) operated by Sapien that ingests contributions, runs the PoQ scoring pipeline, reaches consensus on outcomes, and produces the unlock/slash decisions that the vault then enforces on chain."

So the real architecture is: a web and mobile labeling product (closed source, off chain) produces quality decisions, and a privileged server key (`ENGINE_ROLE`) reaches into an on chain vault to unlock or slash a user's staked collateral. The token exists on chain and is clean. The staking and penalty vault exists on chain and is genuinely well engineered. But the "network", the labeling, the validation, the consensus, the reputation, and the reward accounting, is a centralized off chain system. This is disclosed rather than hidden, which is a transparency credit, but it directly contradicts the louder "everything runs on smart contracts" marketing and it caps how much of the decentralization narrative can be credited to code.

Transparency finding: the core PoQ engine (the actual "decentralized network" claim) is closed source and off chain and therefore unverifiable. Per audit policy, unseen claims about that engine are not credited. What IS open (token plus vault) is credited on its merits.

## Claim 1: A fully on chain, verifiable decentralized network for labeling and rewards

CLAIM: Marketed as a "decentralized network where people label and validate AI training data and earn tokens," with third party writeups stating that on Sapien "your points, rewards, staked tokens, and reputation history are all secured on chain."

REALITY: OVERSTATED. Only staked tokens and the slashing of that stake are on chain. The labeling, the validation, the scoring consensus, and the reward and reputation accounting are off chain in the operator run PoQ engine.

EVIDENCE:
- `docs/SapienVault.md` states the vault "does not score, evaluate, or judge contribution quality on chain" and that "the validation work itself lives off chain in the PoQ engine."
- `AGENTS.md` records the same as a workspace fact: "`ENGINE_ROLE` is held by an off chain server that performs PoQ (proof of quality) validation; the vault is an asset locking mechanism used to slash low quality contributions."
- In `SapienVault.sol`, the only quality related on chain functions are `unlockStake` (line 274) and `slashStake` (line 291), both gated by `onlyRole(ENGINE_ROLE)`. There is no on chain scoring, task, or reputation logic anywhere in the source.
- On chain reality of scale: the live vault holds about 234,269 `SAPIEN` (`totalAssets`), roughly 0.023 percent of supply. The on chain footprint of the "network" is tiny compared to the platform marketing.

IMPACT: A buyer who believes the network's intelligence is trustless smart contract logic is mistaken. The value proposition depends on trusting an off chain operator to score honestly and to call slash and unlock fairly. Decentralization here is aspirational, not implemented.

## Claim 2: Points, rewards, and reputation are secured on chain

CLAIM: "Everything on Sapien runs on smart contracts deployed on Base. That means your points, rewards, staked tokens, and reputation history are all secured on chain."

REALITY: FALSE for points, rewards, and reputation. Only staked tokens (and their slashing) are on chain.

EVIDENCE:
- No rewards distribution contract, points ledger, or reputation registry exists in `sapien-contracts`. The repo contains exactly one product contract, `SapienVault.sol`, plus its types and interface.
- The vault's events are limited to stake lifecycle and admin config: `StakeLocked`, `StakeUnlocked`, `StakeSlashed`, `SharesSlashed`, `MinDepositAgeUpdated`, `MinTrancheSizeUpdated` (see `docs/SapienVault.md` events section). There is no `RewardPaid`, `PointsAccrued`, or `ReputationUpdated` primitive.
- The token bytecode has no reward or reputation hooks; it is a plain capped ERC 20 with permit (see Claim 4).

IMPACT: "On chain reputation and rewards" is not backed by code. Points and rewards are an off chain database the operator controls. This is the sharpest gap between the marketing and the shipped code.

## Claim 3: On chain staking with slashing governs contribution quality

CLAIM: Sapien uses "staking, peer validation, and on chain reputation systems," with token staked as collateral that can be slashed for low quality work.

REALITY: CONFIRMED IN CODE for the staking and slashing half (the enforcement layer). The vault is real, deployed, verified, audited, and used. The peer validation and reputation half is off chain (see Claims 1 and 2).

EVIDENCE:
- `SapienVault.sol` is a full ERC 4626 vault: `ERC4626Upgradeable`, `AccessControlDefaultAdminRulesUpgradeable`, `PausableUpgradeable`, `ReentrancyGuardUpgradeable`, `UUPSUpgradeable` (lines 25 to 32). Shares are `"Sapien PoQ Vault" / vSAPIEN` (line 94).
- `lockStake` (line 257) lets a holder move matured balance into a locked, at risk position; `slashStake` (line 291) burns shares from a slashed user. The slash uses a dilution compensated burn `x = s_D * S / (S + s_D - b)` (`_slashShareAmount`, lines 537 to 546) so the penalty accrues to the other stakers rather than being recaptured by the slashed user. This is a nontrivial, correctly reasoned mechanism.
- Live confirmation on Base: the vault's `asset()` returns the `SAPIEN` token address, `totalAssets` is nonzero (about 234,269 SAPIEN), share supply is nonzero, and the contract is a verified ERC 1967 proxy. Staking is genuinely in use, at small scale.
- Engineering quality is high: three external audit PDFs are committed (`audits/Sapien_Quantstamp_Audit_Report_June_2025.pdf`, `audits/Sapien_Zokyo_Audit_Report_August_2025.pdf`, `audits/QS_SapienVault_Final_Report_June_2026.pdf`), each Quantstamp finding (`SAP-1` through `SAP-7`) has a dedicated regression test under `test/audits/`, and there is a Halmos symbolic suite plus a Foundry invariant suite.

IMPACT: The on chain economic security layer is real and above average in quality. The caveat is that what triggers a slash or unlock is decided off chain, so the honesty of the slashing still reduces to trusting the operator's engine.

## Claim 4: SAPIEN token utility and tokenomics

CLAIM: `SAPIEN` is an ERC 20 on Base that "powers the entire incentive structure" as staking collateral and network currency, fixed 1,000,000,000 supply.

REALITY: CONFIRMED IN CODE for the token's own properties, which are clean and low trust. Utility as staking collateral is confirmed. Utility as reward and governance currency is off chain or unproven (see Claim 6).

EVIDENCE (all from live Base state and bytecode inspection):
- `name` = "Sapien", `symbol` = "SAPIEN", `decimals` = 18.
- `totalSupply` = `maxSupply` = 1e27 (1,000,000,000 tokens). Supply is fully minted and capped.
- No mint capability: the runtime bytecode contains no `mint(address,uint256)` (`40c10f19`) or `mint(uint256)` (`a0712d68`) selector. Supply cannot be inflated.
- No owner and no admin: `owner()` reverts, and there is no `transferOwnership`, `renounceOwnership`, `grantRole`, or `pause` selector. The token is effectively ownerless and immutable.
- Not upgradeable: the EIP 1967 implementation and admin storage slots are both zero, so the token is not a proxy.
- It is an ERC 20 with EIP 2612 `permit` (`d505accf` present) and a hard `maxSupply` cap. Verified on Base as "SapienToken" (Solidity 0.8.30).

IMPACT: For the token itself, smart contract risk is low. There is no mint, no owner, no upgrade, and no pause on the token, so the classic rug vectors (silent mint, ownership abuse, honeypot pause) are absent at the token layer. The token is a fixed supply asset whose price depends on the off chain business, not on token contract trust.

## Claim 5: Millions of contributors and blue chip enterprise clients

CLAIM: "Over 1.7M to 1.9M contributors across 110+ countries," "over one million tasks per day," "80 million labeled items," and "30+ enterprise clients including Alibaba, Toyota, and the United Nations."

REALITY: UNVERIFIED and likely OVERSTATED relative to on chain activity. These are self reported off chain platform metrics with no on chain counterpart, and they are not credited as code facts.

EVIDENCE:
- None of these figures are derivable from chain state. The only on chain participation signal is vault staking, which totals about 234,269 `SAPIEN` (roughly 0.023 percent of supply). Millions of active, economically engaged contributors are not reflected on chain.
- The labeling platform that would substantiate task counts and client logos is closed source and off chain; there is nothing in the public repo to confirm client relationships or throughput.

IMPACT: Traction claims may be real as web platform usage, but they are unverifiable and should not be treated as proof of a decentralized, on chain network. Enterprise client name drops in particular carry no on chain evidence.

## Claim 6: Governed through the on chain protocol on Base

CLAIM: "Governance is managed through the on chain protocol on Base."

REALITY: OVERSTATED. There is no governance contract in the public source, and the only administrative control found is a small multisig over the upgradeable vault.

EVIDENCE:
- The `sapien-contracts` repo contains no governor, voting, or timelock governance contract. The single product contract is the vault.
- The vault's `DEFAULT_ADMIN_ROLE` (and ERC 5313 `owner()`) resolves on chain to `0x5602be03ecffbb85d12b7404d4b38af58277e4cc`, which is a Gnosis Safe (version 1.4.1) with two owners (`0x9a0e...86db`, `0x0c6f...19c9`) and a threshold of 1. A 1 of 2 Safe means either single signer can unilaterally pause, change roles, retune the MEV guard, and, critically, authorize a UUPS upgrade of the custodial vault via `_authorizeUpgrade` (line 628).
- The README and docs themselves recommend that admin "SHOULD" be a multisig behind a `TimelockController`; the live deployment is a 1 of 2 Safe with no timelock in the admin path, so the deployed configuration is weaker than the documented intent.

IMPACT: Governance is administrative, not community on chain governance. Because the vault is UUPS upgradeable and custodial, a single compromised or malicious signer of a 1 of 2 Safe could upgrade the vault logic that holds user stake. This is the highest concrete smart contract risk in the system, and it sits on the vault, not the token.

## Positive Findings

- The token is a genuinely clean, minimal trust asset: fixed 1,000,000,000 capped supply, no mint, no owner, no upgrade proxy, no pause, EIP 2612 permit, verified source. This removes the most common token level rug vectors.
- The on chain staking and slashing vault is unusually well engineered for the sector: a single choke point `_update` for all balance changes, ERC 7201 namespaced storage to reduce upgrade collision risk, a `_decimalsOffset` of 3 to blunt the ERC 4626 inflation attack, per tranche deposit age accounting, and a mathematically justified dilution compensated slash.
- Verification culture is strong and rare: three external audits (Quantstamp June 2025, Zokyo August 2025, Quantstamp Final June 2026), a per finding regression test for every audit item (`SAP-1` through `SAP-7`, plus `S1` to `S3`), a Foundry invariant suite, and a Halmos formal verification suite with honestly documented solver limits.
- Documentation is candid about its own trust assumptions. It explicitly names the off chain PoQ engine as "the protocol's primary off chain trust assumption" and flags engine liveness as a single point of failure rather than hiding it. This honesty is the reason the score is a pass despite an overstated decentralization narrative.
- The token is real and liquid: listed on Coinbase, Binance (November 2025 listing), MEXC, LBank, Phemex, and Aerodrome, with a market cap near 20M USD. Backed by disclosed funding (a 10.5M USD seed led by Variant, with Animoca and Yield Guild Games among earlier backers).

## Conclusion

Sapien is a legitimate, well executed project wearing an oversized decentralization costume. The parts that live on chain (the token and the staking and slashing vault) are among the cleanest in the DePIN sector: the token is immutable, capped, non mintable, and ownerless, and the vault is thrice audited, formally checked, and candidly documented. That earns a passing grade and separates Sapien clearly from the typical "points on a centralized server plus a hype token" pattern.

The gap is in the framing. The core of the advertised "decentralized network", the labeling, the validation, the consensus, the reputation, and the reward accounting, runs off chain on an operator controlled server, and the project's own docs say so. Marketing that "points, rewards, and reputation are all secured on chain" is false; on chain there is only staked collateral and slashing, driven by a privileged off chain `ENGINE_ROLE` key. Governance is a 1 of 2 Gnosis Safe over an upgradeable custodial vault, which is the single most material smart contract risk and is weaker than the project's own documented intent of a multisig behind a timelock. Traction figures in the millions are off chain and unverifiable, and the on chain stake is tiny (about 0.023 percent of supply).

Net: a real product and a clean token, with a decentralization story that the code only partly supports. Score 70 out of 100, Passed, MEDIUM risk. The medium rating reflects the off chain trust dependency and the 1 of 2 upgrade key over the custodial vault, balanced against a low risk, immutable token and high code quality where code actually exists.
