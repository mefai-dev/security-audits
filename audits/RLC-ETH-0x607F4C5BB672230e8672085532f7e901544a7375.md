## Why This Report Exists

Most of the reports we publish document projects whose code contradicts their marketing. iExec is the opposite kind of subject: a project that has shipped working code continuously since 2017. The purpose here is the same discipline applied fairly. We read the actual public source, claim by claim, and we credit what is genuinely implemented while flagging where the marketing runs ahead of the code.

We are not making accusations. We are not spreading FUD. We read the source and show what is actually implemented. iExec earns real credit on this pass: the confidential compute pipeline exists, the Proof of Contribution consensus is real, the staking and slashing are real, and RLC is a genuine fixed supply unit of account. The caveats that remain are honest ones, and they matter for anyone who believes the words "trustless" and "your data stays yours" literally. The largest single caveat is that the confidentiality of every protected dataset rests on a centralized off chain server (the SMS) and on a closed source third party attestation service (Scontain SCONE CAS), not on cryptography or on the blockchain.

## Method

For every major marketing or documentation claim we located the relevant code in the real repositories (PoCo, iexec-sdk, iexec-sms, iexec-worker, tee-worker-compute-rust, dataprotector-sdk, iexec-voucher-contracts), fetched raw files from GitHub, and read what is actually implemented. Each claim below is labelled CONFIRMED IN CODE, OVERSTATED, or FALSE, with a repository path, file, line, and a short verbatim snippet.

## Claim 1: Real confidential computing inside Intel SGX and TDX enclaves

**CLAIM:**
> "TEE-secured off chain computation" and "Encrypted types are executed inside Trusted Execution Environments (TEE)." iExec markets Intel SGX and, more recently, Intel TDX confidential computing.

**REALITY:** CONFIRMED IN CODE. The worker genuinely launches application containers as enclaves and injects the SCONE, Gramine, or TDX runtime parameters, and the Secret Management Service (SMS) only assembles a secret session for deals that carry the TEE tag and are authorized on chain. ## Claim 2: The enclave confidentiality is hardware attested and secrets go only to a verified enclave

**CLAIM:**
> "The CAS attests the quote through Intel Attestation Service and sends the secrets if the MREnclave is as expected." iExec markets confidential computing as a trust minimized guarantee: your secret is released only to the exact enclave measurement you approved.

**REALITY:** OVERSTATED. Three real caveats sit under this claim:

1. The SMS performs no cryptographic attestation itself. It builds a session object and posts it to the proprietary, closed source Scontain SCONE CAS, which is the component that actually attests the quote and releases secrets. There is no DCAP, PCCS, IAS, RA-TLS, or quote parsing code anywhere in the four open repositories.
2. The expected enclave fingerprint (MRENCLAVE) is supplied, not independently verified. For the application enclave it is the value the app owner wrote on chain at app registration (trust on registration). For the pre and post compute enclaves it is a value in the SMS operator's own YAML config.
3. The security posture is operator tunable and can be weakened: the session can tolerate insecure options such as `debug-mode`, `outdated-tcb`, and `hyperthreading`, and can ignore named Intel security advisories.

**EVIDENCE:**
```java
// iexec-sms/src/main/java/com/iexec/sms/tee/session/scone/cas/CasConfiguration.java:27-37
/**
 * CAS: Configuration and Attestation Service.
 * When a service wants to access those secrets, it sends a quote with its MREnclave.
 * The CAS attests the quote through Intel Attestation Service and sends the secrets
 * if the MREnclave is as expected.
 */
```
```java

final TeeEnclaveConfiguration enclaveConfig = taskDescription.getAppEnclaveConfiguration();
...

```
```solidity
// PoCo/contracts/registries/apps/App.sol:20-37

    _initialize(msg.sender);

```
```yaml

```

**IMPACT:** The confidentiality guarantee is real but its root of trust is Intel silicon plus the closed source Scontain SCONE CAS plus a Microsoft Azure attestation endpoint plus whoever configures the SMS, not the blockchain and not pure cryptography. A user cannot independently verify from open source that the exact code they approved ran in a genuine enclave; that link is asserted by proprietary components. High.

## Claim 5: RLC is required to pay for every computation

**CLAIM:**
> "Every confidential computation requires RLC." "To run a task on the protocol ... you need to lock RLC in the protocol during the task period."

**REALITY:** OVERSTATED. RLC is the unit of account (Claim 4), but the code does not require a nonzero RLC payment for a computation to run. Order prices default to zero, the on chain price checks are `>=` comparisons that pass at zero, and the amount locked is the sum of prices times volume, which is zero for a free order. On top of that, two mechanisms let a party other than the requester bear the cost: `sponsorMatchOrders` (anyone pays for a third party) and the VoucherHub (iExec issues non transferable RLC backed credits that are burned to sponsor deals). On Bellecour, gas itself was paid in a bridged, effectively free RLC.

**EVIDENCE:**
```js

createWorkerpoolorder: workerpoolprice = '0'   // L971
```

```solidity
require(_requestorder.workerpoolmaxprice >= _workerpoolorder.workerpoolprice,"iExecV5-matchOrders-0x05");
```

DataProtector itself ships all price ceilings at zero (`dataprotector-sdk/src/config/config.ts`: `DEFAULT_MAX_PRICE = 0`, `MAX_DESIRED_*_ORDER_PRICE = 0`; `grantAccess.ts` `pricePerAccess = 0`).

**IMPACT:** "Every computation requires RLC" is not literally enforced. A zero priced order matches and executes with zero RLC changing hands, and vouchers or sponsors can pay on a user's behalf. The accurate statement is that RLC is the settlement and staking currency when a price is charged, not a hard per task toll. Medium.

## Claim 6: DataProtector keeps your data confidential and under your sole control

**CLAIM:**
> Protect your data so it can be monetized and used in confidential computing without ever being exposed; you choose who can access it, with which apps, and at what price.

**REALITY:** OVERSTATED. The mechanism is real and well built: the file is encrypted client side with AES-256-CBC, the ciphertext is uploaded off chain, and only a schema plus a pointer plus a checksum go on chain as a dataset registry entry. Access is granted through a signed dataset order restricted to a specific authorized app and carrying the TEE tag. The overstatement is in "sole control" and end to end confidentiality: the AES decryption key is uploaded to and custodied by the iExec SMS, and the ciphertext is stored by default on iExec hosted IPFS. Confidentiality therefore rests on the same TEE plus SMS trust model as Claim 2, not on cryptography that keeps iExec itself out. A compromised or malicious SMS holds the key to every protected dataset.

## Claim 7: Decentralized, permissionless worker pools

**CLAIM:**
> Anyone can contribute computing power; decentralized worker pools provide the compute.

**REALITY:** OVERSTATED. Creating a worker pool is permissionless (it mints an ERC-721 in the WorkerpoolRegistry). But which workers actually get to execute a task is not open participation: every contribution must carry an authorization signature from the pool scheduler (the workerpool owner), enforced on chain. For TEE tasks, when the protocol owner has set a TEE broker, the authorizing key is a single iExec controlled address rather than the pool owner. So task admission is gated by the scheduler, and for confidential tasks by an iExec key.

**EVIDENCE:**

## Claim 8: Trustless, immutable smart contracts

**CLAIM:**
> The protocol is enforced by immutable smart contracts; users do not have to trust an operator.

**REALITY:** OVERSTATED. The PoCo core is an upgradeable EIP-2535 Diamond. The owner controlled `diamondCut` can add, replace, or remove any facet, which is full power to change the deployed protocol logic, including escrow and consensus. Configuration setters (`setTeeBroker`, `setCallbackGas`, `configure`) are owner gated. ## Additional Note: The SMS is a trusted server, not an enclave

Worth stating plainly because it compounds Claims 2 and 6. The Secret Management Service that holds every dataset key, app secret, and per task enclave signing key does not itself run inside an enclave. It is a stock JRE process that encrypts secrets at rest with a single AES key read from a file on disk, and it generates and custodies the Ethereum keypair used as the on chain "enclave signer" for each task.

**EVIDENCE:**
```dockerfile
# iexec-sms/Dockerfile:1-15   plain JRE, no sconify / no SGX device
FROM eclipse-temurin:21.0.11_10-jre-noble
ENTRYPOINT ["java","-jar","/app/iexec-sms.jar"]
```
```java
// iexec-sms/src/main/java/com/iexec/sms/encryption/EncryptionService.java:36-47
private byte[] aesKey;   // one symmetric key (encryption.aes-key-path) for all secrets
// TeeChallengeService.java:59-105  getOrCreate -> creates/decrypts the per-task enclave private key
```

**IMPACT:** The party operating the SMS can, in principle, read every protected dataset key and forge enclave result signatures. This is the practical center of gravity of the whole trust model. Low (design disclosure, mitigated in production by running secret release logic against the closed SCONE stack, but not verifiable from open source).

## Conclusion

iExec is a genuine confidential computing project whose code substantially delivers what it markets. The confidential compute pipeline into SGX and TDX enclaves is real (Claim 1). The Proof of Contribution consensus, with staking, replication, rewards, and slashing, is real and audited (Claim 3). RLC is a genuine fixed supply asset and the true settlement and staking currency (Claim 4). Four claims are confirmed in code and none are false or fabricated.

The overstatements are the familiar gap between "trustless" marketing language and a design that, in reality, leans on three centralized or closed elements: a single off chain Secret Management Service that custodies every decryption key, a closed source third party attestation service (Scontain SCONE CAS) plus Intel hardware as the actual root of trust, and an owner upgradeable Diamond governed by an iExec multisig or timelock. "Every computation requires RLC" is not literally enforced (zero priced orders, sponsors, and vouchers all execute), "permissionless worker pools" is gated at the execution layer, and "your data stays yours" is custodial in the sense that iExec's SMS holds the key.

None of this is fraud. It is the difference between a strong, working confidential computing platform with real hardware and operator trust assumptions, and the frictionless trustless narrative the marketing sometimes implies. Score 78 out of 100, LOW to MEDIUM RISK, driven entirely by trust and centralization caveats rather than by any dishonest or broken code.
