## Executive Summary

iExec is one of the older live projects in the space, an ICO era 2017 confidential computing effort, and this audit finds that its onchain reality mostly matches what it sells. The token contract is genuinely clean, the RLC utility is real and enforced by protocol code, and the confidential compute tooling is shipping rather than sitting behind a landing page. The caution here is not a broken product or a fake token. It is a live protocol repositioning underneath the marketing, and traction that the project does not quantify.

1. **The token is genuinely clean and audited.** RLC verifies live as iEx.ec Network Token, 9 decimals, with a fixed total supply near 86,999,785 RLC. The bytecode contains no mint function, no pause, and no proxy, and OpenZeppelin audited it in 2017 with no severe findings. Supply cannot inflate, transfers cannot be frozen, and the logic cannot be silently swapped.
2. **The utility is real and enforced by contract.** RLC is the protocol unit for payments, staking as sRLC, escrow, and worker deposits, enforced by the PoCo contracts. Running a task locks RLC and distributes payment to the app provider, the protected data provider, and the worker. This is not a purely speculative token.
3. **The confidential compute tooling is live.** DataProtector, Web3Mail, Web3Telegram, the iApp Generator and TEE workers are documented, published as maintained npm packages updated through 2026, and deployed on Arbitrum One, with active GitHub development into mid 2026.
4. **The core marketplace is mid transition.** The Bellecour sidechain that hosted the classic worker pool compute marketplace was officially sunset on July 2, 2026, forcing RLC holders to migrate, and the repositioning toward confidential DeFi and TEE tooling on Ethereum and Arbitrum is very recent. The specific decentralized marketplace as historically marketed is being restructured rather than scaled.
5. **Traction is mixed and partly opaque.** RLC and its contracts are trivially verifiable onchain, but iExec does not publish headline counts of tasks executed, deals settled, or active workers, and named integrators appear without hard usage figures. Market capitalization sits near 22.7 million USD on roughly 2.9 million USD daily volume, which is modest.

The result is a genuine, long running project with a clean token and real utility, carrying real transition and traction risk rather than a scam. This lands iExec at 74 out of 100, Passed, at MEDIUM overall risk.

## 2. On chain Security Assessment (MEFAI analysis)

MEFAI's direct read of the RLC contract on Ethereum returned:

| Check | Result |
|-------|--------|
| Token identity | iEx.ec Network Token, RLC, 9 decimals, verified |

| Transfer state | Permanently enabled, `locked()` returns false and no relock function exists |

**Interpretation.** At the contract level RLC is among the cleanest tokens MEFAI reviews. It is a fixed supply StandardToken with no mint, no pause, no proxy, and no fee on transfer, backed by a 2017 OpenZeppelin audit that found nothing severe. The only residual contract level notes are the nonzero Ownable owner, which is a dormant crowdsale contract with no power to mint, pause, or refreeze, and the nonstandard 9 decimals that integrators must account for. Neither is a red flag. The reasons this project carries MEDIUM overall risk sit at the product transition and traction level below, not in the token contract.

## 3. Claim vs Reality: "Build with Programmable Privacy"

> Site: The iExec homepage leads with "BUILD WITH PROGRAMMABLE PRIVACY" and states that, "Powered by TEE and Intel TDX, iExec delivers full control over on chain and off chain data confidentiality," offering a "Plug and play privacy toolkit" that lets developers "Bootstrap TEE in minutes."

**Reality: this one is largely delivered.** Unlike many privacy narratives, the tooling behind this headline is real and inspectable. The confidential computing stack, DataProtector, Web3Mail, Web3Telegram, the iApp Generator, and TEE workers, is documented at docs.iex.ec, published as maintained npm packages (iexec 9.0.1, @iexec/dataprotector, @iexec/web3mail, all updated in 2026), and deployed on Arbitrum One. The GitHub organization shows active pushes across the SDK, worker, market API and explorer through mid 2026. A developer following this claim reaches working code, not an error page. MEFAI credits this as a genuine, shipped capability.

## 4. Claim vs Reality: "The entire iExec decentralized confidential computing ecosystem"

> Site: iExec presents RLC as the fuel of a "decentralized confidential computing ecosystem," stating that "Every confidential computation requires RLC. Every piece of protected data consumed requires RLC," with payment "automatically and transparently distributed" to the app provider, the protected data provider, and the worker.

**Reality: the utility is real, but the decentralized marketplace is mid transition.** The token economics are genuinely enforced by the PoCo contracts: running a task locks RLC as sRLC, uses staked deposits as economic security for correct execution, and pays out in RLC on completion. That part is true and verifiable. The important nuance is structural. The classic worker pool compute marketplace ran on the Bellecour sidechain, which was officially sunset on July 2, 2026, forcing RLC holders to migrate, and the project has repositioned toward programmable privacy and confidential DeFi on Ethereum and Arbitrum. So the decentralized compute marketplace as historically marketed is being restructured rather than scaled, even as the underlying TEE tooling works today. Buyers should read the ecosystem language as a live protocol in transition, not a settled, high throughput marketplace.

## 5. Claim vs Reality: Fixed Supply "Written in Stone"

> Site: iExec documentation states that "RLC has a maximum supply of 87 million tokens, ensuring scarcity and value preservation," that "The entire circulating supply of RLC has already been minted," and public materials describe the supply as written in stone with no admin access to the ERC 20 contract.

**Reality: the substance holds, the wording is slightly loose.** MEFAI's onchain read confirms the practical claim: the bytecode contains no mint function, so supply cannot inflate, and the fixed total sits near 86,999,785 RLC created at deployment in 2017. Strictly speaking the "no admin access" phrasing is imprecise, because the contract still exposes a nonzero Ownable owner set to the old crowdsale contract. That owner, however, cannot mint, cannot pause, and cannot refreeze transfers, since its only token power was a one time unlock that has already been used and there is no relock function. The gap between the marketing and the code is cosmetic wording, not a hidden power. Credited as an honest supply claim.

## 6. Claim vs Reality: Adoption and the $30T Opportunity

> Site: iExec markets "Confidential DeFi & RWA" as its 2026 direction, cites that "up to $30T in financial assets could ultimately move on chain" pending confidentiality, states it is "Live on Arbitrum" with builders such as Otomato and ApeBond, and names alliances with Aethir, AR.IO, ChainGPT and DeXe.

**Reality: real integrators, unquantified traction, and a forward looking market frame.** The named builders and partners appear to be real and the Arbitrum deployment is genuine. What is not verifiable from public sources is operational scale. iExec does not publish headline counts of tasks executed, deals settled, or active workers, the explorer at explorer.iex.ec is a client side application rather than a statistics dashboard, and historical Bellecour data is being wound down. RLC market capitalization near 22.7 million USD on roughly 2.9 million USD daily volume indicates real but modest usage. The "$30T" figure is a total addressable market frame for the opportunity, not a metric of what iExec processes today. The direction is credible and the tooling is live, but adoption should be treated as early and unproven until the project publishes usage figures.

## 11. Conclusion

iExec is a genuine, long running confidential computing project whose token holds up well to scrutiny. The RLC contract is a clean, fixed supply, audited StandardToken with no mint, no pause, no proxy, and no fee, and its utility as the protocol unit for payments, staking, and worker deposits is real and enforced by code. The confidential compute tooling is live on Arbitrum rather than aspirational. What holds the score at 74 out of 100 and the risk at MEDIUM is not a contract flaw or a fake product, it is a protocol in transition and a traction picture the project keeps opaque: the Bellecour marketplace was sunset in July 2026 and RLC holders were forced to migrate, the Arbitrum repositioning is recent, and iExec publishes no headline usage figures to back its ecosystem claims. On balance the token and the technology are legitimate, so the verdict is Passed, with the caution centered on execution of the transition and on adoption that remains to be proven.

## 12. Recommendations

**For the iExec team:**
- Publish concrete post migration usage figures (tasks executed, deals settled, active workers on Arbitrum) so the ecosystem claims can be independently verified.
- Tighten the "no admin access" language to reflect that a dormant Ownable owner still exists, even though it cannot mint, pause, or refreeze.
- Give clear, dated milestones for the Bellecour to Arbitrum transition so holders and integrators can plan the migration.
- Continue documenting the TEE tooling as thoroughly as it is today, since that is a genuine strength.

**For users:**
- Treat RLC as a legitimate fixed supply utility token whose contract is clean and audited, with supply that cannot inflate.
- Understand that the compute marketplace is mid transition after the Bellecour sunset, and confirm you are holding or bridging RLC on the correct chain.
- Account for the nonstandard 9 decimals in any integration or accounting.
- Verify adoption and integration claims independently, since operational usage numbers are not published.
