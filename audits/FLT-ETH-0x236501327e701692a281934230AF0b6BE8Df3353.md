## Executive Summary

Fluence markets FLT as the token behind a cloudless CPU and GPU compute marketplace, and the audit finds that this is one of the rarer cases where the product is genuinely live and the token has real structural utility. The Ethereum contract broadly confirms the tokenomics story, the compute service is operational, and FLT staking actually gates provider capacity. The gaps are honest ones: an upgradeable proxy that the tokenomics page does not disclose, headline pipeline numbers that run well ahead of what is staked onchain today, and a token price that has collapsed roughly 99.9 percent from its all time high.

1. **The compute product is real and shipped.** Fluence runs a live console at console.fluence.network with open sign up, a documented API, developer docs at fluence.dev, a CPU marketplace live since the 2024 mainnet, and GPU containers launched on 3 October 2025 with real posted pricing. This is a working service, not vaporware.
2. **FLT utility is genuine, not decorative.** Under the Proof of Capacity model every provider CPU core must carry roughly 200 USD equivalent of staked FLT to be activated, with slashing for missing proofs. The token is real collateral and coordination for the network, though customers pay in stablecoins rather than FLT.
3. **The token contract is clean today but upgradeable.** RPC reads confirm a fixed one billion FLT genesis supply, 18 decimals, and no active mint, burn, pause, or transfer fee. The one material technical nuance is that FLT is a UUPS upgradeable proxy whose logic can be replaced by a 2 of 4 Gnosis Safe multisig, so the fixed supply is a policy of the current code rather than a permanent guarantee, and the site does not disclose this.
4. **Pipeline runs ahead of onchain reality, and price has collapsed.** Marketing cites 250,000 CPU cores under letters of intent and 170 million USD of FLT stake to onboard them, but only about 25 million FLT is actually staked onchain, and live dashboard metrics are not independently verifiable. FLT has fallen about 99.9 percent from a 2.43 USD peak to near 0.0013 USD, with a market cap around 460K.

The contract is not a scam and the utility is real, which keeps Fluence out of the flagged tier. The residual risks are upgrade authority, forward looking figures presented near live metrics, and a severely depressed token market. This lands Fluence at 70 out of 100, Passed.

## 2. On chain Security Assessment (MEFAI analysis)

MEFAI's direct read of the FLT contract on Ethereum returned:

| Check | Result |
|-------|--------|
| Token identity | Fluence, FLT, 18 decimals, verified canonical address |
| Total supply | 1,000,000,000 FLT (raw 1000000000000000000000000000), matches the marketed one billion cap |
| Mint authority | None in the active implementation; supply created at genesis |
| Burn | None in the active implementation |
| Pause authority | None; `paused()` reverts and no pause selector is present |
| Upgradeable | Yes, UUPS ERC1967 proxy delegating to implementation `0xbdb50eff425fb2b1b67fea21b8420eeb6d99ccc0` |
| Owner / upgrade key | Gnosis Safe version 1.3.0 multisig at `0x7f629403fdcc02ad83aa5debd1d4b1548982afac`, threshold 2 of 4 signers |
| Transfer fee | None |
| Governance | OpenZeppelin voting style ERC20 consistent with a Governor and DAO model |

**Interpretation.** Day to day, FLT behaves as a plain, fixed supply ERC20 with voting: one billion tokens, 18 decimals, no active minting, burning, pausing, or fee on transfer. The residual contract risk is not hidden logic in the current code but the standing ability of the owner multisig to upgrade that code. Because the token is a UUPS proxy, the 2 of 4 Gnosis Safe can replace the implementation and could, in principle, introduce minting, pausing, or fees that do not exist today. The fixed supply is therefore a property of the deployed implementation rather than an immutable guarantee, and the tokenomics page does not mention the proxy or the upgrade key. This is a centralization and disclosure caution, not a red flag, because no marketing copy claims immutability.

## 3. Claim vs Reality: "Slash your AI cloud bill by up to 85%"

> Site: "Slash your AI cloud bill by up to 85%. Run training, inference, AI agents, and general purpose services on CPU and GPU cloud, with predictable pricing, zero egress fees, and no lock in."

**Reality: this is largely delivered, and credited.** The cloudless compute marketplace is a genuine working product. Fluence runs a live console at console.fluence.network with open sign up, a documented API to search capacity and deploy virtual machines, GPU containers and bare metal, and public developer docs at fluence.dev. The CPU marketplace has been live since the 2024 mainnet, and GPU containers launched on 3 October 2025 with real posted pricing, including 2.56 USD per hour for an NVIDIA H200 versus the higher rates the site attributes to incumbent providers. The project names real infrastructure providers such as PiKNiK, GlobalStake, Kabat, Nebula, and Spheron, and real customers such as Antier, Zeeve, AR.IO, dKloud, and Nodes Garden, which is stronger than typical DePIN marketing. The honest caveat is maturity: parts of the console still carry an Alpha VM testing program label, and the broader ambition of matching hyperscaler scale remains early. The core service, however, is operational and billing real usage.

## 4. Claim vs Reality: "Total Token Supply: 1,000,000,000 FLT"

> Site: "Total Token Supply: 1,000,000,000 FLT."

**Reality: confirmed onchain, with one undisclosed nuance.** A live RPC read returns a totalSupply of exactly one billion tokens at 18 decimals, created at genesis in February 2024, and market trackers list total and max supply both at one billion, so there is no supply above the stated cap and no active mint function to inflate it. Ongoing growth in circulating tokens comes from vesting unlocks of existing supply, not new issuance, which is consistent with the disclosed vesting schedule. The nuance a due diligence reader should carry forward is that the deployed contract is not a simple immutable ERC20 but a UUPS upgradeable proxy. The fixed one billion is the behavior of the current implementation, and the owner multisig retains the ability to change that logic later. The claim is accurate as stated; it simply understates the upgrade authority that exists in the deployed code.

## 5. Claim vs Reality: "Stake FLT to secure the network" and Proof of Capacity

> Site: "Stake FLT to secure the network and earn rewards while supporting decentralized cloud growth." "Every CPU core must receive FLT stake on it to be activated." "The protocol target is $200 of FLT tokens to be staked per each core added."

**Reality: real, structural token utility.** This is the strongest part of the Fluence story and it is credited in full. FLT staking is not a cosmetic reward program; it gates the network's supply side. Under the Proof of Capacity model, provider hardware must carry roughly 200 USD equivalent of staked FLT per core to be activated, activated hardware submits proofs validated on the Fluence chain, providers and delegators earn FLT for valid proofs, and insufficient proofs lead to slashing. That makes FLT genuine collateral and coordination for compute capacity rather than a purely speculative asset. The fair caveat is that customers pay for compute in stablecoins, not in FLT, so the token is the provider side security bond and governance instrument rather than the direct spend currency. The framing that FLT pays for compute is therefore indirect, but the underlying utility is real and enforced by the protocol.

---

## 6. Claim vs Reality: "250,000 CPU cores under signed LOIs" and "$170 million stake in FLT"

> Site: "At launch, Fluence had 250,000 CPU cores under signed LOIs (Letters of intent) and our waitlist has grown by an additional 600,000 cores. Onboarding all this hardware will require $170 million stake in FLT."

**Reality: forward looking pipeline, modest onchain reality.** These are the headline numbers, and they describe intent rather than activated capacity. Letters of intent and waitlists are commitments to onboard hardware in the future, not compute that is live and staked today. Against the 170 million USD of FLT stake implied by the pipeline, only about 25 million FLT is actually staked onchain, a wide gap between the marketed capacity and the deployed collateral. The live statistics application is a dashboard whose real time compute unit and provider counts could not be independently confirmed from public data. Alongside these forward looking figures, the project does cite plausible early traction, including over one million USD in CPU marketplace annual recurring revenue, an eight million plus pipeline, and 3.5 million USD in customer savings. The concern is presentation: large pipeline and letter of intent numbers sit close to live metrics, and a reader should treat the 250,000 cores and 170 million USD stake as aspiration, not activated supply.

---

## 7. Claim vs Reality: Buyback and Deflationary Framing

> Site: Several Fluence articles emphasize a revenue funded FLT buyback and deflationary token dynamics.

**Reality: an open market policy, not a contract level burn.** There is no burn function in the active token implementation, so any buyback reduces circulating float by having the treasury hold repurchased tokens acquired on the open market rather than by destroying supply at the contract level. That is a legitimate economic policy, but it is discretionary and reversible, not a hard coded deflation. This distinction matters more given the token's market condition: FLT has fallen roughly 99.9 percent from a 2.43 USD all time high to near 0.0013 USD, with a market cap around 460K and thin liquidity. A buyback described as deflationary should be read as a treasury behavior subject to funding and discretion, not as an automatic supply reduction guaranteed by the code.

## 8. Positive Findings (Credited)

- The compute marketplace is a genuine, operational product with a live console, open sign up, a documented API, and developer docs, spanning CPU (live since 2024), GPU containers (launched October 2025), and bare metal.
- FLT has real structural utility: the Proof of Capacity model requires roughly 200 USD of staked FLT per provider core to activate capacity, with slashing, plus DAO governance under a Governor model.
- The token contract is clean in its active implementation: a fixed one billion genesis supply, 18 decimals, and no mint, burn, pause, or transfer fee.
- Real named providers and paying customers, plus roughly one million USD in CPU marketplace annual recurring revenue, indicate traction beyond marketing.
- The canonical FLT address is verified on major trackers and matches the onchain reads.

## 12. Conclusion

Fluence is a real decentralized compute network with a live product and a token that does genuine work. The compute marketplace is operational across CPU, GPU, and bare metal, FLT staking structurally gates provider capacity under the Proof of Capacity model, and the deployed contract confirms a clean fixed one billion supply with no active minting, pausing, or transfer fee. Those facts keep the project well clear of the flagged tier. The report scores Fluence 70 out of 100, Passed, because the residual risks are real but bounded: the token is a UUPS upgradeable proxy whose logic a 2 of 4 multisig could change, the tokenomics page does not disclose that upgrade authority, the marketed 250,000 core pipeline and 170 million USD implied stake run far ahead of the roughly 25 million FLT actually staked, and the token has fallen about 99.9 percent from its all time high into a small, thin market. The caution here is not a contract exploit or a fake product, it is a genuine network whose forward looking claims and depressed market warrant careful reading.

## 13. Recommendations

**For the Fluence team:**
- Disclose on the tokenomics page that FLT is a UUPS upgradeable proxy and name the 2 of 4 multisig that holds the upgrade key, so readers do not assume an immutable ERC20.
- Consider a timelock on the upgrade key or a higher multisig threshold to reduce the concentration of upgrade power.
- Clearly separate forward looking pipeline and letter of intent figures from live activated capacity and staked FLT, and publish verifiable onchain stake alongside dashboard metrics.
- If describing buybacks as deflationary, clarify that there is no onchain burn and that float reduction depends on treasury discretion and funding.
- Continue moving console features out of the Alpha testing program toward general availability.

**For users:**
- FLT has genuine staking utility, since Proof of Capacity requires staked FLT to activate provider cores, and the compute product is live; still, treat the 250,000 core pipeline and 170 million USD stake figures as intent rather than delivered capacity.
- Understand that the token is a UUPS upgradeable proxy, so a 2 of 4 multisig can replace the logic and could add minting, pausing, or fees that do not exist today.
- Note that FLT has fallen roughly 99.9 percent from its all time high into a small, thin market, and independently verify staking totals and dashboard metrics before relying on them.
