## Executive Summary

Deeper Network is one of the more grounded projects in this batch. Unlike many DePIN narratives, the hardware is genuinely real and genuinely for sale, and the DPR token has real designed utility inside a working mining and credit system. The reasons this still lands as Flagged are concentrated in the token contract and in a handful of marketing and identity issues, not in the product itself.

1. **The hardware DePIN is real and sold.** Deeper Connect devices, including the Mini at 399 dollars and the Air at 349 dollars, are sold through a standard Shopify store at shop.deeper.network and also through third party retail such as Amazon. This is a shipping hardware business, not a whitepaper.
2. **The token is actually used, not purely speculative.** DPR feeds a Proof of Credit system where users stake DPR (roughly 5,000 to 100,000 DPR) to raise a credit score and earn mining rewards, with 60 percent of supply reserved for mining. That is genuine utility, though it lives natively on the Deeper Chain, and the Ethereum ERC 20 audited here is a mirror of that native asset.
3. **The contract is centralized in a way the site never discloses.** The verified source exposes an owner only mint function capped at 10 billion (about 3.4 billion of headroom remains), a pause switch that can halt all transfers, and a freeze account function that can block any address from sending or receiving. These powers sit behind a Gnosis Safe multisig, which is a mitigant, but they are not surfaced anywhere in the public marketing.
4. **Headline traction is hardcoded and not independently verifiable.** The 200,000 plus nodes claim is static marketing copy rather than a live feed, and third party DePIN trackers show no live node figure to corroborate it. Separately, the frontend carries no token address at all, so a buyer must confirm the canonical DPR contract independently and beware a circulated lookalike that actually resolves to the DENT token.

The contract is not a scam and the business is real, but the freeze and pause powers, the undisclosed mint headroom, and the unverifiable traction claim keep DPR at 48 out of 100, Flagged.

## 2. On chain Security Assessment (MEFAI analysis)

MEFAI's direct read of the DPR contract on Ethereum returned:

| Check | Result |
|-------|--------|
| Token identity | Deeper Network, DPR, 18 decimals, verified |
| Total supply (Ethereum) | ~6.6 billion DPR |
| Max supply | 10,000,000,000, hard cap enforced in source |
| Mint authority | Present, `mint(uint256)` onlyOwner, capped at 10 billion, roughly 3.4 billion of headroom remains |
| Pause authority | Present, onlyOwner, currently not paused |
| Freeze authority | Present, `freezeAccount` onlyOwner, can block any address from sending or receiving |
| Ownership | Not renounced; owner in storage slot zero is `0xb90182c7a8639de5ffd3f831009365d98971270f`, a Gnosis Safe v1.3.0 multisig |
| Upgradeable | No, implementation slot empty, not a proxy |
| Transfer fee | None |
| Compiler | Solidity 0.4.24 (aged toolchain), internally named "Token" |

**Interpretation.** At the identity level DPR is sound: verified, hard capped, no transfer fee, and not upgradeable. The residual contract risk is centralization, and here it is broader than a typical mint plus pause setup. The owner can mint up to the cap, pause all transfers, and, most notably, freeze any individual account. A freeze or blacklist power means the owner can unilaterally block a specific holder from moving tokens, which is a stronger control than mint or pause alone. These powers are held behind a multisig rather than a single externally owned key, which is a real mitigant, but none of them are disclosed in the public marketing. The aged Solidity 0.4.24 compiler and the generic internal contract name are minor additional notes.

## 3. Claim vs Reality: "Over 200,000 nodes worldwide, the largest node network globally"

> Site: The marketing states the network has over 200,000 nodes worldwide across many countries, forming the largest node network globally.

**Reality: a hardcoded figure with no live corroboration.** MEFAI's frontend review confirmed that the 200,000 nodes figure is static copy embedded in the page rather than a live counter or feed. Third party DePIN trackers that list Deeper show placeholder loading states rather than an actual node count, so there is no independent number to confirm or refute the claim. The underlying network and devices are real, but the specific traction headline should be treated as marketing until backed by a verifiable live metric.

---

## 4. Claim vs Reality: "A fixed 10 billion supply with 60 percent reserved for mining"

> Site and docs: DPR has a fixed maximum supply of 10 billion, with 60 percent of tokens allocated to mining rewards and halving as mining progresses.

**Reality: the cap claim holds, but the token is still mintable to it.** The verified ERC 20 source does enforce a hard 10 billion ceiling, so the headline cap is accurate and the 60 percent mining allocation is consistent with the emission design. The live Ethereum total supply reads about 6.6 billion, below the roughly 10 billion figure that market aggregators display, a gap explained by ongoing emission and by DPR circulating natively on the Deeper Chain rather than only on Ethereum. The caveat is that the owner can still mint the remaining headroom, roughly 3.4 billion DPR, up to the cap. The cap is real, but "fixed supply" in the marketing sense understates that meaningful issuance authority remains active.

---

## 5. Claim vs Reality: "DPR powers staking, credit score and mining rewards"

> Site and docs: DPR is the fuel of a Proof of Credit network, where users stake DPR to raise a credit score and earn mining rewards, and where DPR pays for services such as advertising and dApp hosting.

**Reality: this is largely true and is the project's strongest point.** The Proof of Credit design is real and documented: users can raise a credit score organically by sharing bandwidth or by staking DPR (roughly 5,000 to 100,000 DPR), and a higher credit score yields higher mining rewards, with 60 percent of supply set aside for that program. DPR also has described roles in paying for ads and hosting dApps. This is genuine, shipped token utility rather than a speculative promise, which distinguishes Deeper from many DePIN peers. The one clarification for holders is that this staking, credit, and mining activity lives on the native Deeper Chain, while the asset audited here is the Ethereum ERC 20 mirror, so the ERC 20 itself is primarily a bridged or mirrored representation rather than the direct staking instrument.

---

## 6. Claim vs Reality: "A decentralized, trustless privacy network" versus owner mint, pause and freeze

> Site: Deeper is positioned as a decentralized, trustless privacy and Web 3.0 infrastructure owned by its users.

**Reality: the token contract carries strong owner controls that the site never mentions.** The verified source exposes three privileged owner functions: mint up to the 10 billion cap, pause to halt all transfers, and freeze account to block any specific address from sending or receiving. A freeze or blacklist function is the sharpest of these, because it lets the controller single out and immobilize an individual holder's balance. All three sit behind the Gnosis Safe v1.3.0 multisig at `0xb90182c7a8639de5ffd3f831009365d98971270f`, so no single key holds them, which reduces but does not remove the concern. The gap that matters for transparency is that a project marketed as decentralized and trustless does not disclose anywhere on its public site that a multisig can pause the whole token or freeze any account. Users should understand these powers exist and are currently active, even though the token is not paused today.

---

## 7. Claim vs Reality: token identity and the DENT lookalike

> Context: DPR is frequently referenced by contract address, and one address in circulation is commonly attached to the project.

**Reality: the canonical DPR must be confirmed by hand, and a lookalike address is a different token.** MEFAI verified that the canonical DPR contract is `0xf3AE5d769e153Ef72b4e3591aC004E89F48107a1`, confirmed by its onchain name Deeper Network, symbol DPR, and 18 decimals, and corroborated by CoinGecko and Deeper support documentation. A separately circulated address, `0x3597bfD533a99c9aa083587B074434E61Eb0A258`, does not belong to Deeper at all: it returns the name and symbol DENT with 8 decimals and a roughly 100 billion supply, which is the Dent Wireless token. Compounding this, the Deeper marketing site carries no token address whatsoever, so a buyer gets no on site anchor to the correct contract and must verify DPR independently. The absence of a drainer or fake token widget on the site is a positive for safety, but the address ambiguity is a real acquisition risk.

## 8. Positive Findings (Credited)

- The hardware DePIN is real and commercially available: Deeper Connect devices sell through a standard Shopify store at shop.deeper.network and through third party retail, and the frontend review found no wallet drainer, no connect wallet flow, and no malicious scripts.

- The token contract is a verified, hard capped, non upgradeable ERC 20 with no transfer fee, and the canonical address is confirmable onchain.
- Privileged roles sit behind a Gnosis Safe v1.3.0 multisig rather than a single externally owned key.

## 12. Conclusion

Deeper Network is a real hardware DePIN with a token that is actually used, which is why its product risk is low and why it scores above the weakest projects in this batch. It still earns a Flagged verdict at 48 out of 100 because the concerns sit in the token contract and in transparency. The owner multisig can mint roughly 3.4 billion DPR to the cap, pause every transfer, and freeze or blacklist any individual account, and none of these powers are disclosed on a site that markets the network as decentralized and trustless. On top of that, the headline 200,000 plus nodes figure is hardcoded and independently unverifiable, the site shows no token address, and a lookalike address in circulation actually resolves to the unrelated DENT token. The caution here is not a broken product or a scam contract, it is a genuine business wrapped in a token whose centralization and marketing outrun what is disclosed.

---

## 13. Recommendations

**For the Deeper team:**
- Disclose the mint, pause, and freeze account powers and the controlling multisig prominently in the token documentation; a decentralized positioning should not hide a freeze or blacklist capability.
- Publish the canonical DPR contract address on the official site and warn about the DENT lookalike, so buyers are not left to verify by hand.
- Replace the hardcoded 200,000 plus nodes figure with a live, verifiable network metric, or clearly label it as a point in time estimate.
- Consider a timelock on the privileged owner functions and a path toward reducing or renouncing the freeze power.

**For users:**
- Confirm the canonical DPR contract `0xf3AE5d769e153Ef72b4e3591aC004E89F48107a1` before any purchase, and do not trust `0x3597bfD533a99c9aa083587B074434E61Eb0A258`, which is the DENT token, not DPR.
- Understand that the owner multisig can pause all transfers and freeze any account, even though the token is not paused today.
- Treat the 200,000 plus nodes claim as marketing until a live figure is available, while noting that the hardware and store are real and the DPR mining and credit utility is genuine.
