## Executive Summary

aixbt by Virtuals is one of the more genuine products in the AI agent token category, and this report credits that plainly. Where the audit lands it at Flagged is not the product, it is the token contract and the way the token relates to the product.

1. **The core intelligence product is real and live.** The aixbt.tech site is a working read only market intelligence and rankings application, serving topic and project rankings, cluster and momentum views, an Intel feed, and an apps directory. Its live metrics are served by the first party backend at api.aixbt.tech rather than hardcoded, and the frontend contains no wallet drainer surface, no address swap, and no fabricated audit badges. The rankings product is genuinely shipped and used.

2. **The token carries an undisclosed live transfer tax.** MEFAI's onchain read confirms a one percent tax on buys and a one percent tax on sells, both set to one hundred basis points, routed to a project controlled tax recipient. Many listing and marketing pages describe AIXBT as a standard agent token and omit this tax entirely, so buyers pay a real cost that the public descriptions do not surface.

3. **Ownership is a single externally owned key that can change the tax.** The owner is an externally owned account that has not been renounced. Within the limits of the shared AgentToken template it can adjust tax parameters and exclusions. A single key controlling the fee on every trade is a centralization and transparency concern even where the template caps the range.

4. **Token utility is thin and concentrated in one product.** The token's practical role is holder gating, with access to the AIXBT Terminal requiring on the order of 600,000 AIXBT. The intelligence itself is the value, the token mostly gates the premium tier and carries speculation, and the entire token thesis rests on the fortunes of a single AI agent product with high volatility.

The contract is not a scam, supply is effectively fixed and nearly fully circulating, and the product is real, which keeps overall risk at MEDIUM. But the hidden fee, the non renounced externally owned owner that can move that fee, and the thin token utility weigh a genuinely live product down to 50 out of 100, Flagged.

## 2. On chain Security Assessment (MEFAI analysis)

MEFAI's direct read of the AIXBT contract on Base returned:

| Check | Result |
|-------|--------|
| Token identity | aixbt by Virtuals, symbol AIXBT, 18 decimals, verified by RPC and multiple explorers |
| Total supply | 998,914,867 AIXBT, close to a one billion genesis with a tiny burn, consistent with no inflation |
| Mint authority | No accessible public mint function found; supply appears fixed at genesis |
| Owner | `0xe220329659d41b2a9f26e83816b424bdacf62567`, an externally owned account with no code, not renounced |
| Upgradeable | Minimal proxy (EIP 1167 style 45 byte clone) delegating to shared implementation `0x082cb6e892dd0699b5f0d22f7d2e638bbada5d94` |
| Transfer tax | Yes, one hundred basis points on buy and one hundred on sell, recipient `0x8e0253da409faf5918fe2a15979fd878f4495d0e`, routing to a project vault |

**Interpretation.** The supply side of AIXBT is reassuring: effectively fixed, nearly fully circulating, no accessible mint path, and a token identity that matches the launch story of a Virtuals AgentToken clone. The concerns sit in two places. First, the contract charges a live tax on both sides of every trade, which is a real and recurring cost rather than a one time event. Second, control of that behavior rests with a single externally owned key that has not been renounced and can adjust tax parameters and exclusions within the template limits. Two getters, the max supply cap and the liquidity pool reference, reverted when read against the proxy, so hard cap enforcement and liquidity handling are inferred from the shared template and from market data rather than confirmed by a direct call. None of this is an exploit, but it is materially more centralization and fee exposure than the public listings communicate.

## 3. Claim vs Reality: "A live AI market intelligence agent and terminal"

> Site: aixbt is presented as an AI driven crypto research agent with a rankings product and a terminal that turns market data into actionable intelligence.

**Reality: this claim holds up.** The aixbt.tech site is a functioning read only rankings and intelligence application. It serves topic rankings, project rankings, cluster analysis, momentum tracking, an Intel feed, and an apps directory, and its live figures are pulled from the first party backend at api.aixbt.tech rather than being hardcoded into the page. The frontend review found no transaction or signature primitives, no approvals or permits, no address swap or lookalike, and no fabricated certification badges, so the marketing story and the shipped product match. This is a genuinely live product, and the audit credits it as the project's strongest point.

---

## 4. Claim vs Reality: "A standard agent token" versus the hidden transfer tax and externally owned owner

> Site and listings: AIXBT is widely described as a one billion supply Base AI agent token, with most pages presenting it as a plain fixed supply token and no mention of fees.

**Reality: there is a live, undisclosed transfer tax under single key control.** MEFAI's onchain read confirms buy and sell tax basis points both set to one hundred, meaning a one percent charge on buys and a one percent charge on sells, routed to the project tax recipient `0x8e0253da409faf5918fe2a15979fd878f4495d0e` and on to a project vault. Many marketing and aggregator descriptions omit this tax, so a buyer relying on those pages does not learn that every entry and exit pays the project. Compounding this, the owner is an externally owned account, `0xe220329659d41b2a9f26e83816b424bdacf62567`, which has no code and has not been renounced, and within the shared AgentToken template that key can change tax parameters and exclusions. A fee that is real, undisclosed on most listings, and adjustable by one key is the central transparency and centralization finding of this report. For fairness, this one percent buy and sell tax is the standard default of the Virtuals AgentToken template that every agent token on that platform carries, so it is a known platform feature rather than a bespoke or malicious surprise, but it remains a real cost that most third party listings do not surface.

## 5. Claim vs Reality: "AIXBT powers the product"

> Site and community framing: holding AIXBT is presented as the way to access the agent's premium intelligence.

**Reality: utility is thin and reduces to holder gating.** The token's practical function is access control. Reaching the AIXBT Terminal requires holding on the order of 600,000 AIXBT, and beyond that gate the token's role is largely speculation. The intelligence product itself is the value, and much of the public rankings surface is readable without the token, so AIXBT is a premium access pass and a bet rather than a settlement asset or a working fuel of the product. Value is concentrated in the success of a single AI agent, which brings high volatility and single product risk. The utility is real but narrow, which is a meaningful part of why a live product does not score higher here.

---

## 6. Claim vs Reality: "Traction and adoption"

> Site and market: aixbt is presented as a leading AI agent with real usage and reach.

**Reality: the traction is real, with concentration caveats.** aixbt is one of the recognized AI agent products in the category, with an active following, a live terminal behind the holder gate, and a market capitalization that has ranged into the tens of millions of dollars. Unlike projects whose flagship is unreachable, aixbt's flagship works. The caution is that adoption and value are concentrated in one product and its narrative, the model's outputs are a black box to users, and the project has weathered a prior security incident, so traction should be read as genuine but concentrated rather than broad and diversified.

## 11. Conclusion

aixbt by Virtuals is a case where the product is real and the token is the problem. The rankings and intelligence application is genuinely live, served by a first party backend, and shipped as a clean read only frontend with no drainer surface, which is more than many projects in this category can show. That is why overall risk stays at MEDIUM rather than higher. What holds the score to 50 out of 100 and lands the verdict at Flagged is the token layer: a live one percent buy and one percent sell tax that most listings do not disclose, a non renounced externally owned owner that can move that tax with a single key, and a token whose real utility is limited to holder gating for the terminal while value concentrates in one volatile product. The caution here is not that aixbt does not work, it is that a buyer of AIXBT takes on an undisclosed recurring fee and single key control for a token whose usefulness is thinner than the product's reputation suggests.

---

## 12. Recommendations

**For the aixbt team:**
- Disclose the one percent buy and one percent sell tax prominently on the site and in listing metadata, so buyers see the real cost of entry and exit.
- Move ownership from a single externally owned key to a multisig or a timelock, and cap or renounce the ability to change tax parameters, so the fee cannot be altered by one key.
- Clarify the token's role beyond terminal gating and speculation, or stop implying the token powers the product when the product mostly stands on its own.
- Publish the max supply and liquidity handling in a directly verifiable way, since those getters reverted on the proxy.

**For users:**
- Understand that buying and selling AIXBT costs one percent on each side, paid to the project, even where listings do not mention it.
- Understand that a single non renounced key can adjust that tax within the template limits.
- Treat AIXBT as a concentrated bet on one AI agent product, with thin token utility beyond terminal access, and size positions accordingly.
