## Why This Report Exists

A prior thin listing scored this asset 71 and treated it, implicitly, as a token of the "Milady AI" ecosystem. A 71 invites a reader to assume product substance: an AI network, a utility token, a codebase that the coin plugs into. The reference material pointed at `github.com/milady-ai/milady`, which is indeed a real and busy AI project.

The purpose of this report is to separate the two things that share the name. There is a genuine AI product called Milady, and there is a small `MILADY` token on BNB Smart Chain. The question is whether they are the same thing in code, whether the token carries any of the product's substance, and whether the token itself is safe to hold. We answer all three from primary sources: the live contract, the verified source, the liquidity pool, and the repository, not from the coin's marketing.

The short version: the token is a clean, renounced, liquidity burned meme with no honeypot behavior and no product utility. It borrows the identity of an unrelated (in code) AI project. It is not a scam, and it is not infrastructure. A score in the low fifties reflects both facts honestly, and corrects the earlier 71 downward because that number credited product substance that the token does not carry.

## The Foundation: a Launchpad Clone Called FlapTaxToken

The token contract holds almost no code of its own. Its runtime bytecode is 45 bytes:

```
0x363d3d373d3d3d363d7329e6383f0ce68507b5a72a53c2b118a118332aa85af43d82803e903d91602b57fd5bf3
```

That is a textbook EIP-1167 minimal proxy. Every call is delegated to the implementation address embedded in the middle of it, `0x29e6383f0ce68507b5a72a53c2b118a118332aa8`. The implementation is verified on Sourcify (exact match, verified 2026-05-21) as `FlapTaxToken`, a shared template used by the "Flap" bonding curve launchpad on BSC. In plain terms: this coin was minted by a factory that presses out identical tokens, and the individual token holds nothing but a pointer to the shared logic.

The template is an OpenZeppelin based `ERC20PermitUpgradeable` plus `OwnableUpgradeable`, extended with a launchpad tax and a bonding curve state machine. Its `initialize` function mints the entire `maxSupply` of `1e9 ether` (one billion tokens) once, to the deployer, and there is no other mint path anywhere in the source. The state machine has five states, from `BondingCurve` through migration to `TaxEnforced` and finally `TaxFree`.

Because the token is a minimal proxy with the implementation address baked into immutable bytecode, it is not upgradeable. There is no proxy admin, no `upgradeTo`, and the implementation itself calls `_disableInitializers()` in its constructor. What is deployed is what will always run.

Everything below tests the token against the identity it presents.

## Claim 1: This Is a Milady AI Token

CLAIM

> The token's own IPFS metadata sets `"website":"https://github.com/milady-ai/milady"` and `"twitter":"https://x.com/milady_bsc"`. DexScreener lists its website as `https://milady.ai/`. The presentation is that `MILADY` on BSC is the coin of the Milady AI project.

REALITY: OVERSTATED. The AI project is real, and it is genuinely unconnected to the token in code. `github.com/milady-ai/milady` is an actively developed "local first" AI assistant built on elizaOS, MIT licensed, written in TypeScript with Bun. It has thousands of commits. It has no token, no tokenomics, no contract address, no presale, and no reference to `MILADY` or to `0xc20E45...7777` anywhere. Its own BNB Smart Chain support is a feature for trading other people's meme tokens through PancakeSwap, driven by a user supplied `EVM_PRIVATE_KEY`; it is not powered by, gated by, or settled in this token. The `milady.ai` site presents the software ("Local-First Control"), not a coin sale.

So the pointer runs one way. The token points at the AI project; the AI project never points back. A buyer who reads "Milady AI" and infers that this coin is the economic layer of that software is inferring something the software does not claim and the contract cannot deliver.

EVIDENCE: token metadata resolved from the on chain `metaURI` CID `bafkreiezffut2nshj5lk2tjwnkunqteplh5lgd2wlkmcjz4wcm65k3cdca`; repository read of `github.com/milady-ai/milady`; the implementation source `FlapTaxToken.sol`, which contains only ERC20, permit, ownable, tax, and migration logic and no AI, oracle, or product hooks.

IMPACT: reputational borrowing. The safety of the coin is fine (see the positives), but its association with a substantive AI product is not reciprocated. This is the single largest gap between presentation and reality, and it is why the earlier 71 was too generous.

## Claim 2: A Token With Utility

CLAIM

> Implied by naming an AI product: that holding or spending `MILADY` does something inside that product, that it is a utility token rather than a bet on a name.

REALITY: OVERSTATED. There is no utility, on chain or in the product. The contract is a standard transferable token with an ERC20 permit and a launchpad tax. It has no staking, no access control tied to the AI software, no burn for service, no revenue share to holders, and no callback into any product. The AI assistant runs on large language model API keys (Anthropic, OpenAI, Ollama and similar) and never consults this token. Framed honestly, this is a pure meme coin that names an AI project, not an AI utility token.

EVIDENCE: full function set of the verified `FlapTaxToken` implementation (standard ERC20 plus `permit`, `nonces`, `DOMAIN_SEPARATOR`, `owner`, `renounceOwnership`, `transferOwnership`, and the tax and migration functions); repository review showing no token integration.

IMPACT: the correct mental model is a name bet. Buyers should price it as a meme, not as software with cash flows.

## Claim 3: A Fair, Community Launch With No Team Overhang

CLAIM

> Implied by a bonding curve launchpad launch: broad distribution, no privileged team allocation waiting to dump.

REALITY: CONFIRMED IN CODE, with one caveat. The distribution is genuinely broad for a micro cap. The declared creator wallet `0x16C44E575437dD84ea75e4E0100A717D081E6fF4` holds only about 1.11 percent of supply. About 44.87 percent of supply sits inside the PancakeSwap pool as tradeable liquidity, and the remainder is spread across roughly 5,534 holders. Critically, the liquidity provider tokens for the main pool are 100 percent held by the dead address, meaning the liquidity is burned and cannot be withdrawn by anyone. The caveat is the tax, addressed next.

EVIDENCE: live `balanceOf` reads on the token for the creator, the pool, the tax splitter, and the dead address; live `balanceOf` and `totalSupply` reads on the pair contract showing 56,508.56 of 56,508.56 liquidity provider tokens at `0x000000000000000000000000000000000000dEaD`.

IMPACT: the classic exit vectors, a team supply dump and a liquidity pull, are both weak here. The creator holds little, and the liquidity is unremovable.

## Claim 4: A Clean, Low Fee Token

CLAIM

> The presentation of a simple meme coin implies little or no friction on trading.

REALITY: OVERSTATED. There is a real, active 1 percent tax on every buy and sell that touches the main pool, and it is effectively permanent. The live `taxRate` is 100 basis points. The token is in state 3, `TaxEnforced`, so `FlapTaxToken._getTax` applies `amount * taxRate / 10000` to any transfer involving `mainPool`. The collected tax is periodically swapped to `WBNB` and sent to the launchpad splitter `0xfe82d692ce53d6d3a82059118e02e4b139e04ac6`. The tax has an expiry, but `TAX_DURATION` was set to 3,153,600,000 seconds, about 100 years, so `taxExpirationTime` lands in the year 2126. In practice the fee never sunsets.

The redeeming detail is that this fee is immutable in the safe direction. `taxRate` is written only once, in `initialize`, and the only other assignment in the code sets it to zero after expiry. There is no owner function to raise it, and the owner is renounced anyway. So the 1 percent cannot be turned into 30 percent; it is a fixed, small, perpetual toll to the launchpad, not a dial the deployer can crank.

EVIDENCE: live reads `taxRate` = 100, `state` = 3, `taxExpirationTime` = 4925026557, `TAX_DURATION` = 3153600000, `taxSplitter` = `0xfe82d692ce53d6d3a82059118e02e4b139e04ac6`; source of `_getTax`, `_transfer`, and `_liquidateTax` in `FlapTaxToken.sol`; independent simulation measuring about 1 percent on both buy and sell.

IMPACT: low but permanent friction. Every round trip loses roughly 2 percent to the fee before price impact. It is not a rug vector, but it is a standing cost that a "clean meme" framing hides.

## Positive Findings

These are credited, and they are the reason the verdict is a pass rather than a flag.

1. Ownership renounced. `owner()` returns the zero address. The only functions that were ever owner gated are `startMigration` and `finalizeMigration`, both of which only push the pool state forward; the token has already migrated, so nothing of value is left uncallable. CONFIRMED IN CODE.
2. Liquidity burned. 100 percent of the main pool's liquidity provider tokens are at the dead address. No party can pull liquidity. CONFIRMED IN CODE.
3. No mint after launch. The single `_mint(msg.sender, maxSupply)` runs once inside an `initializer` guarded function. Supply is fixed at one billion. There is no owner mint, no inflation. CONFIRMED IN CODE.
4. No pause, no blacklist, no freeze. A full source review and a bytecode selector scan find none of these. No address can be blocked from selling, and trading cannot be halted. CONFIRMED IN CODE.
5. Not upgradeable. An EIP-1167 minimal proxy with an immutable implementation. The logic cannot be swapped. CONFIRMED IN CODE.
6. Not a honeypot. An independent simulation reports the token is not a honeypot, with buy and sell tax near 1 percent, transfer tax zero, and every sampled sell (1,757 of 1,757) succeeding, zero failed, zero siphoned, highest observed tax 2.15 percent. CONFIRMED via live simulation.
7. Verified source. The implementation is open source and verified on Sourcify, so the behavior above is auditable rather than assumed. CONFIRMED.

## Conclusion

Milady on BSC (`0xc20E45E49e0E79f0fC81E71F05fD2772d6587777`) is a clean, renounced, liquidity burned meme coin produced by the Flap launchpad from its shared `FlapTaxToken` template. On the safety axis it is better than most tokens that carry a passing grade: ownership is renounced, the liquidity is burned to the dead address, there is no mint after launch, no pause, no blacklist, no freeze, and no upgrade path, and an independent simulation confirms it is not a honeypot. The one live risk mechanism, a 1 percent tax, is small, cannot be raised, and flows to the launchpad rather than to a discretionary team wallet.

What it is not is a Milady AI token in any functional sense. The real `github.com/milady-ai/milady` software is a legitimate, active, MIT licensed AI assistant that has no token, does not reference this contract, and does not use it. The coin borrows that identity in one direction only. There is no product utility, no code linking the token to the software, and the market is tiny, roughly $18,900 in market cap on thin liquidity.

Judged fairly, this is a safe meme with no substance, not infrastructure and not a scam. That is why the score is 54 and not 71: the earlier grade credited an AI product that the token does not carry. It is a pass on contract safety, with an explicit warning that any purchase is a bet on a name, carrying a permanent 1 percent toll and the ordinary total loss risk of a micro cap meme.
